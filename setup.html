Setting it up

About a minute, once. Nothing to install but the app.

First, check if your phone qualifies: a Samsung Galaxy Z Flip or Z Fold running Android 15 (One UI 7) or newer. The trackpad needs the phone to offer its outer screen to apps while it is open. The Google Pixel Fold and newer Pixel folds have the same mode and Google Play offers the app on them; all four ran in our lab tests.

Steps

  1. Install Cover Screen Trackpad from Google Play, then open it.

    Cover Screen Trackpad on Google Play

  2. Turn on its accessibility service. Tap the button at the top of the app, choose Installed apps → Cover Trackpad, switch it on and tap Allow. This is how the pointer moves and clicks.
  3. Open the phone fully and tap Start trackpad. The cover screen becomes the trackpad, the pointer appears on the main screen, and a small floating button shows while it is on. Go to any app.

Next time, just launch the app. Close the phone, turn the screen off, tap the floating button or choose Turn off in the menu to stop it.

Coming from an earlier version? Versions before 1.1 needed a companion app called Shizuku. Version 1.1 does not use it, and there is nothing to set up in developer options. If you installed Shizuku only for Cover Screen Trackpad, you can remove it.

If something is not working

I launched the app but there is no pointer

Check the status line at the top of the app — it says what is missing. The usual cause is that the phone is not fully open.

“This phone does not offer its outer screen to apps”

The phone's software does not allow it, and Cover Screen Trackpad cannot work on it. Please send a problem report from the app, so we know.

Finger trails on the cover screen, but no pointer on the main screen

Android has hidden the pointer. The app notices and fixes this by itself within a couple of seconds. If the pointer still does not appear, turn the trackpad off and launch it again. If that does not help either, go to Settings → Accessibility → Installed apps → Cover Trackpad, switch it off and on again, then launch Cover Screen Trackpad.

The pointer will not click a permission pop-up

Android blocks that on purpose, so no app can tap Allow for you. Use the screen directly for those, then carry on.

Does this drain the battery?

Barely. Cover screens are OLED, where a black pixel is simply switched off, and the trackpad lights very little of one: a few words, and a trail that follows your finger while you are actually using it. The cost sits inside the ordinary noise of a day’s use. A larger cover screen has more panel to light, so a Fold costs a little more than a Flip.

PRO switches the words and the animation off, which leaves the panel black — quieter to be seen using, as well as very slightly cheaper to light.

If you want to be thorough, the idle fade-out setting turns the trackpad off automatically when you stop using it, and pointer memory brings the pointer back where you left it.
