Cover Screen Trackpad

The cover-screen trackpad for foldables.

Your foldable's cover screen is facing away from you while the phone is open. This turns it into a rear trackpad: slide a finger on the back, a pointer moves on the front, tap to click.

Both screens of a Galaxy Z Fold 8 side by side, recorded in a device lab: a touch on the cover screen (right) moves the pointer on the main screen (left), and two circles drawn on the cover open the menu. No sound.

Will it work on my phone? Works on Galaxy Z Flip and Z Fold, and on the Pixel Fold and newer Pixel folds. Setup is one switch.

Why a trackpad on the back?

A big inner display is awkward one-handed — the far corner is always just out of reach, and stretching for it is how phones get dropped. A pointer solves the reach problem the way it does on a laptop, and the back of the phone is where your fingers already are.

It also does things a touchscreen cannot: precise placement of a text cursor, hovering before you commit, and clicking a link the size of a full stop.

And it can turn a swipe into a tap. Switch on edge-click scrolling, park the pointer against the bottom of the screen, and a tap scrolls the page — no dragging, no second hand, nothing held down. If swiping is painful or unreliable for you, that is the difference between reading a long page and giving up on it.

That makes it a reading aid as much as a pointer: hold the phone in one hand, move the pointer and edge-click your way down a long article, and nothing ever covers the text you are reading.

How it works

  1. Open the phone and launch the app. The cover screen becomes active — that's the trackpad surface.
  2. Use any app you like. The trackpad stays on while you browse, read or type.
  3. Drive from the back. Slide to move, tap to click, hold to drag. Draw two circles for a menu with Back, Home and Recents. With PRO, two fingers scroll and pinch.
  4. Close the phone, or turn the screen off. The trackpad turns off, and folding behaves exactly as it normally does.

Requirements

  • A Samsung Galaxy Z Flip or Z Fold, running Android 15 (One UI 7) or newer. The trackpad uses Android's own way of showing a page on the outer screen while the phone is open, so what matters is the software version, not how new the handset is.
  • Tested by hand on the Galaxy Z Flip 7, the Z Fold 7, and a 2021 Z Fold 3 updated to One UI 7. Also ran in a device lab, with scripted touches rather than a hand, on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, Z Flip 8 and Z TriFold.
  • Google Pixel: the Pixel Fold and newer Pixel folds have the same mode, and Google Play offers the app on them. The Pixel Fold, Pixel 9 Pro Fold and Pixel 11 Pro Fold ran in our lab tests, with scripted touches rather than a hand.
  • Nothing else to install. Setup is one switch: Android's accessibility permission for the app.

Questions

Do I need to install anything else?

No. Earlier versions needed a companion app called Shizuku to wake the cover screen; this one asks Android directly, so setup is a single switch: the accessibility permission. If you installed Shizuku for an earlier version, Cover Screen Trackpad no longer uses it and you can remove it.

Why does it need accessibility access?

Moving a pointer and clicking on your behalf is only possible through that API — it is the same route any mouse-emulation tool takes. It is a powerful permission and you are right to be careful with it.

It looks only at which windows are open, never at what is in them. Whether a keyboard is up, so edge-click scrolling switches off while you type. Whether an app or split screen is in front, so the menu offers the right choices. And that something changed, so the floating button can step out of the way and the app can check it still has the cover screen.

It does not read the text on your screen, and nothing it sees is logged, stored or sent anywhere. The app does list the internet permission, which arrives with Google's billing library and is used only to ask Google Play whether you have bought PRO.

Will it drain my battery?

Barely. Cover screens are OLED, where a black pixel is simply switched off, and the trackpad lights very little of one: a few words, and a trail that follows your finger while you are actually using it. The cost sits inside the ordinary noise of a day's use. A larger cover screen has more panel to light, so a Fold costs a little more than a Flip.

PRO switches the words and the animation off, which leaves the panel black — quieter to be seen using, as well as very slightly cheaper to light. There is also an idle fade-out you can switch on, which turns the trackpad off when you stop touching the cover screen; it starts at never, and the slider sets the wait anywhere from 3 to 30 seconds.

Does it work on a Pixel Fold, or a Motorola Razr?

Pixel: yes. Everything rests on the phone offering its outer screen to apps while it is open. The Pixel Fold and newer Pixel folds do, and Google Play offers the app on them. Cover Screen Trackpad ran on the Pixel Fold, Pixel 9 Pro Fold and Pixel 11 Pro Fold in our lab tests, with scripted touches rather than a hand. The Pixel 10 Pro Fold was not run, but sits between two that passed.

Motorola Razr: no. We bought a Razr+ and measured it: it has no mode that runs both screens at once, so the app cannot work on it, and no version of ours can add that. Other makers' foldables are unknown to us; if you have one, a problem report from the app tells us what it offers.

Does folding still work normally?

Yes. The trackpad turns off when you close the phone, when the screen turns off, when you tap the floating button, or when you choose Turn off in the menu — and once it is off, your phone folds exactly as it always did.

Can I try PRO before paying?

Yes — every PRO feature is on for your first seven days: two-finger scroll, pinch and rotate, the full menu and gestures, the active zone, no floating button, the Quick Settings tile and pointer styles. The week begins the first time the trackpad actually comes up on the cover screen rather than at install.

Afterwards the trackpad carries on working — the PRO features switch off and the free ones never expire. You are not charged at the end and there is nothing to cancel. The one thing the week does not include is switching off the cover-screen message; that is yours when you buy.

Is it a subscription?

No. The free version is genuinely usable, and PRO is a single payment. No ads, no accounts, no recurring charge.
