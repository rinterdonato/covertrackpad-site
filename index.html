Cover Screen Trackpad

The cover-screen trackpad for foldables.

Your foldable's cover screen is facing away from you while the phone is open. This turns it into a rear trackpad: slide a finger on the back, a pointer moves on the front, tap to click.

Will it work on my phone? In testing now, coming to Google Play. Works on Galaxy Z Flip and Z Fold. Setup takes about five minutes.

Why a trackpad on the back?

A big inner display is awkward one-handed — the far corner is always just out of reach, and stretching for it is how phones get dropped. A pointer solves the reach problem the way it does on a laptop, and the back of the phone is where your fingers already are.

It also does things a touchscreen cannot: precise placement of a text cursor, hovering before you commit, and clicking a link the size of a full stop.

How it works

  1. Open the phone and launch the app. The cover screen becomes active — that's the trackpad surface.
  2. Use any app you like. The session keeps running while you browse, read or type.
  3. Drive from the back. Slide to move, tap to click, hold to drag. With PRO, two fingers scroll and pinch.
  4. Fold it, or turn the screen off. The session ends and your phone folds exactly as normal.

Requirements

  • A Samsung foldable — Galaxy Z Flip or Z Fold. Verified by hand on Flip 3, Flip 5, Flip 6, Fold 3 and Fold 7. Note the Flip 3 is a 2021 phone: what matters is the software version below, not how new the hardware is.
  • Android 15 (One UI 7) or newer. Earlier versions either lack the dual-screen mode entirely, or give it up every time you switch apps — which leaves the cover screen flickering rather than working.
  • Shizuku, a free companion app. This is the only hard dependency.

Why Shizuku? While a foldable is open, Android switches the cover screen off completely and hides it from every app. Shizuku lets Cover Screen Trackpad ask the system to power both panels — the same mode the camera's dual preview uses. There is no way around it, and no other app can grant it. Setting it up takes a few minutes, once.

That mode is a Samsung addition rather than part of standard Android, so non-Samsung foldables cannot run Cover Screen Trackpad at all. That is a hardware limit and not something a future update can fix. If you try it on a phone without the mode, the app says so plainly rather than failing silently.

Questions

Do I really have to install Shizuku?

Yes, and there is no way around it. While a foldable is open, Android switches the cover screen off and hides it from every app — no permission or setting changes that. Shizuku lets the app ask the system to power both panels, which is the mode the camera's dual preview already uses. It is free, open-source, does not need root, and it is a one-time setup. The setup guide walks through it.

Why does it need accessibility access?

Moving a pointer and clicking on your behalf is only possible through that API — it is the same route any mouse-emulation tool takes. It is a powerful permission and you are right to be careful with it.

It notices two things about your screen, and nothing else. Whether a keyboard is up, so edge-click scrolling switches off while you type. And that something changed — not what changed: Android quietly takes the cover screen back when apps come and go, which would end your session mid-use, so any change is a cue to check and take it back if needed.

It does not read the text on your screen, and nothing it sees is logged, stored or sent anywhere. The app does list the internet permission, which arrives with Google's billing library and is used only to ask Google Play whether you have bought PRO.

Will it drain my battery?

Barely. Cover screens are OLED, where a black pixel is simply switched off, and a trackpad session lights very little of one: a few words, and a trail that follows your finger while you are actually using it. The cost sits inside the ordinary noise of a day's use. A larger cover screen has more panel to light, so a Fold costs a little more than a Flip.

PRO switches the words and the animation off, which leaves the panel black — quieter to be seen using, as well as very slightly cheaper to light. There is also an idle fade-out that ends a session when you stop touching it.

Does it work on a Pixel Fold, or a Motorola Razr?

No, and we would rather say so than sell you something that cannot work. Everything rests on the phone exposing a mode that powers both screens at once. We bought a Razr+ and measured it: no such mode exists on it. The underlying reason generalises — standard Android only describes which single display is currently primary, and running both at once is a Samsung extension on top of that. So Pixel Fold and every other non-Samsung foldable are almost certainly in the same position.

This is hardware, not effort, and no future version will change it. If a non-Samsung foldable ever ships that mode we would be glad to hear about it.

Does folding still work normally?

Yes. A session ends when you close the phone, when the screen turns off, when you tap Stop on the notification, or when you swipe the app away — and ending a session hands fold behaviour straight back to the system.

Is it a subscription?

No. The free version is genuinely usable, and PRO is a single payment. No ads, no accounts, no recurring charge.
