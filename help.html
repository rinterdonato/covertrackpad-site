Cover Screen Trackpad 1.1, by June8 LLC. The full guide; the app carries the short version.

What is Cover Trackpad?

Cover Trackpad turns the cover screen of a foldable into a trackpad while the phone is open. Your finger on the cover screen moves a pointer on the main screen; taps click whatever is under it.

Requires a Samsung Galaxy foldable — the Z Flip series (clamshell) or the Z Fold series (book-style) — running Android 15 (One UI 7) or newer, or a Google Pixel fold. Cover Trackpad needs the phone to offer its outer screen to apps while it is open. The Google Pixel Fold and newer Pixel folds have the same mode, Google Play offers the app on them, and all four ran in our lab tests. The Motorola Razr does not have it and cannot run Cover Trackpad — this was measured on a Razr+, not assumed. Other makers' foldables are unknown. A touchscreen on the outside is necessary but not sufficient.

One-time setup

Open the app, tap the button at the top and switch on Cover Trackpad's accessibility service (Installed apps → Cover Trackpad, then Allow). That is all: nothing else to install. Earlier versions needed a companion app, Shizuku; this one does not use it, so you can remove Shizuku if you installed it only for Cover Trackpad.

Your first week is PRO

Everything is unlocked for seven days, starting the first time the trackpad actually comes up — not when you install it, and the settings screen shows how many days are left.

When the trial ends the app keeps working. You lose the PRO extras and keep the trackpad itself: pointer movement, tap, drag and long-press, edge-click scrolling, the menu's Back, Home, Recents, app drawer, settings and turn off, fade-out when idle, pointer size and speed. The small floating button stays on screen while the trackpad is on; PRO can hide it. Nothing you set up disappears — if you unlock PRO later, it all comes back exactly as you left it.

The features marked PRO in the settings are the ones that stop after the trial. The two most noticeable are two-finger scroll and pinch and the active zone, so if the app suddenly feels different after a week, that is what changed — not a fault.

Daily use

With the phone fully open, launch Cover Trackpad. The cover screen becomes the trackpad, the pointer appears on the main screen, and a small floating button shows while it is on. Switch to any app and control it from the back of the phone:

• Swipe — move the pointer. Slow is precise; a quick flick travels further. That speed-up is a checkbox: turn Speed up with fast swipes off for steady, predictable movement, which is easier to aim if your hands are unsteady.
• Tap (quick, no movement) — click at the pointer.
• Hold still, then release — long-press (context menus, link previews).
• Hold still, then move — drag. The pointer turns green when drag is armed, and the phone gives a tick unless you have turned Vibrate off (it starts on). To move a home-screen icon, keep holding until it lifts, then move. With swipe-to-type enabled in your keyboard's settings, gliding in drag mode over the keyboard types words.
• Two-finger swipe — scroll. Content follows your fingers; the pointer turns blue and stays put. Keep swiping for long pages — the invisible scrolling finger re-grabs on its own.
• Two-finger pinch — zoom in or out around the pointer (maps, photos, browsers). Twisting your fingers rotates, in apps that support it.
• Draw two circles in one go (or a spiral), with one finger — opens the menu. The phone buzzes as soon as the circle is recognised: lift to open the menu, or carry on to the edge of the cover screen to cancel. See The menu below.
• Scratch quickly back and forth, four strokes or more along one line, at any angle — runs the action chosen for it in the settings (Back, to start with). PRO.
• Edge-click scrolling (on by default; slide its slider in the settings all the way left to turn it off) — pin the pointer flat against a screen edge and tap: Cover Trackpad swipes instead of clicking. Bottom edge scrolls down, top scrolls up, left/right page through books. The slider sets how far each swipe travels. The swipe is made across the middle of the screen rather than at the edge you clicked, so it isn't swallowed by a toolbar, a bottom bar or a mini-player sitting there; that also means the longest slider settings level off at around three quarters of the screen. It switches itself off while the keyboard is open, so edge taps near the keys stay clicks. This is also the only way to scroll that needs a single finger, no path and no timing — so if a two-finger swipe is awkward for you, use this instead.

The menu

Two circles drawn on the cover screen open the menu on the main screen. As the pointer passes over a row it lights up and the phone ticks, so you know what a tap will do before you tap. Tap beside the menu to close it.

Free: the navigation row (Back, Home, Recents — on a Samsung with PRO, hold Recents for split screen; it uses Samsung's two-finger swipe for split screen, which has to be switched on in the phone's Multi window settings) and the app row (app drawer, Cover Trackpad's settings, turn off). The app drawer key appears only with launchers it has been tested on (Samsung's One UI Home and Nova). PRO adds rows you choose in the settings: notifications, quick settings and screenshot; split screen (or back to full screen when you are in split screen); flashlight; lock screen; power menu.

With gesture navigation there are no on-screen buttons for a pointer to press, so the menu is how you go Back or Home without touching the main screen.

Turning the trackpad off

• Close the phone, or
• turn the screen off, or
• tap the floating button, or
• choose Turn off in the menu, or
• launch Cover Trackpad again, with Launch as on/off switch enabled in the settings (PRO), or
• just stop touching the cover screen — the pointer fades as a warning, and then the trackpad turns off. Touch the cover screen while they are fading to keep the trackpad on. The Fade out after slider sets the wait, from 3 to 30 seconds, or all the way right for never. At the short end the trackpad becomes something you switch on for a moment rather than leave running. Bear in mind it counts from when you last touched the cover screen, so at 3 seconds the trackpad turns off soon after you take your finger off, and you relaunch to get it back.

With a short fade-out, the trackpad turns off and on again often — so Remember pointer spot brings the pointer back where you left it instead of starting in the middle of the screen every time. Set how long it remembers — off, 15, 30, 45 or 60 seconds, or always. Past about a minute away the old spot is usually stale, and a pointer that reappears somewhere unexpected is worse than one in the middle; always is there for anyone who wants it to behave like a desktop mouse and never re-centre. That is what makes a short fade-out painless — the trackpad can turn off as often as it likes without ever costing you your place.

The Quick Settings tile (PRO) is the fastest way in and out: add it with the checkbox in the settings, then pull down the shade and tap it to turn the trackpad on or off without leaving the app you are in. The tile always shows the real state, so it never claims the trackpad is on after the phone has folded or the screen has slept.

To get to the settings screen at any time, long-press the Cover Trackpad icon on your home screen or in the app drawer and choose Settings — that works whether the trackpad is running or not. While the trackpad is on, the menu's settings key and a long press on the floating button get you there too.

Everything shuts down cleanly and folding behaves normally again. After the screen turns off, relaunch Cover Trackpad to turn the trackpad back on. Leaving the app with Home or Back keeps it running. While the trackpad is on, Android shows its own "Dual screen is on" notification; that is normal.

Automation: make the gesture do anything

Set a drawn gesture's action to Automation app and the gesture sends a broadcast that MacroDroid, Tasker, Automate or anything similar can catch. From there it can do whatever your phone can do — including things with no app-level control of their own, such as Samsung Modes and Routines.

The action to listen for is (the name is older than the gestures, and kept so existing macros keep working):

com.june8llc.covertrackpad.THREE_FINGER_TAP

It carries one extra, source, saying which gesture sent it.

In MacroDroid, leave the trigger's extras empty. Add an Intent Received trigger, type the action above, and add nothing else. Adding an extra row — even a blank one — stops the trigger matching, and it fails silently with nothing in MacroDroid's log to explain why.

Troubleshooting

• No pointer after launching — check the status line at the top of the settings screen: the phone may not be fully open.
• "This phone does not offer its outer screen to apps" — the phone's software does not allow it, and Cover Trackpad cannot work on it. Please send a problem report from the app, so we know.
• Pointer vanished on its own — the screen slept, the phone was closed, or the idle fade-out ran; each turns the trackpad off. Relaunch to start again.
• Finger trails on the cover screen, but no pointer on the main screen — Android has hidden the pointer. Cover Trackpad notices and fixes this by itself within a couple of seconds. If the pointer still does not appear, turn the trackpad off and launch it again. If that does not help either, go to Settings → Accessibility → Installed apps → Cover Trackpad, switch it off and on again, then launch Cover Trackpad.
• The pointer will not click permission dialogs, and cannot be seen over them — that is Android, not a fault, and there is no way around it. Android deliberately refuses injected taps over permission prompts and similar protected windows, and hides overlays like our pointer while they are showing. The protection exists so that an app cannot draw over a permission dialog and tap Allow on your behalf. Use the screen normally for those, then carry on.
• Something else — send a problem report with the button in the settings. It opens your email app with the details written out; nothing is sent until you press send.
